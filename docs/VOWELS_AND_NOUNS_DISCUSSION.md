# Neva morphology follow-up: vowel simplicity and noun ablaut

Status (2026-10-26): D5's *closed list of historically motivated noun irregulars* is now **approved and implemented**, starting with `podos` (foot). D1b masculine plural, D2 feminine dative and the overall no-diphthong policy remain **under discussion**. No other noun changes have been approved.

## D1b: replace diphthongal masculine nominative plural?

Original -oes proposal was rejected. Current -oi (vulkoi) has now been questioned too because of /oi/.

- A (recommended for discussion): **-es** -> vulkes. PIE had athematic animate nominative plural *-es; borrowing it for Neva thematic nouns would be analogical regularization.
- B: **-i** -> vulki. Compare later Greek monophthongization of oi > /i/.
- C: retain **-os** -> vulkos singular and plural. Simple, but removes visible number distinction.

No change to -oi unless approved.

## D2: feminine noun declension

Recommendation: keep the existing paradigm except simplify dative -ai > -e and -ais > -es. This is historically inspired by Greek ai > /e/, and makes the spelling fully phonetic.

| Case | Singular candidate | Plural candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | mathera | matheras |
| Accusative | matheram | matherans |
| Genitive | matheras | matheron |
| Dative | **mathere** | **matheres** |

Current forms remain matherai and matherais. Alternative: dative matheri/matheris from an ai > i simplification. Do not merely pronounce written -ai as /e/: that would violate Neva spelling conventions.

## M/N shared cases (D4 approved)

D4 approved shared M/N genitive and dative *behavior*, not necessarily their eternal spelling. Existing -oi/-ois and -osio also contain adjacent vowels. Future discussion may cover a single-vowel replacement shared by both genders (e.g. -i/-is for dative, or -oso for genitive). Do not change D4's agreement or the dictionary without a separate vote.

## D5: approved closed class; concrete regular vs. irregular examples

Regular working paradigm:
- patheros / patherosio / patheroi (father: nominative/genitive/dative).
- podos / pedosio (foot: nominative/genitive) is now an **explicit exception**, no longer regular.
- vodrom / vodrosio (water: nominative/genitive).

Historical PIE showed inherited stem alternation:
- Father: *ph2ter (nominative) vs. *ph2tr- (oblique); Greek pater/patros, Latin pater/patris. Hypothetical Neva **patheros / pathrosio**, with contracted oblique *pathr-*.
- Foot: PIE *pods (strong nominative *pōds) vs *ped-* oblique; hypothetical Neva **podos / pedosio**. This would change our existing *pedos* headword.
- Water: PIE *wodr- vs weak *ud-*, sometimes with an r/n-stem alternation; retaining Neva *vodr-* everywhere is deliberately much easier.

Possible policies:
1. No irregular noun stems.
2. **ADOPTED:** a *closed, historically supported* list of irregular inherited nouns, never applied productively to new nouns; explicit strong and oblique stems stored in dictionary. Currently **one entry**: `podos`, strong `pod-` for nominative/accusative and weak `ped-` for genitive/dative.
3. Productive noun-ablaut classes for new vocabulary (not recommended).

Except for the approved `podos/ped-` pattern, the forms above remain **proposed Neva inventions**, not reconstructed PIE declensions. The difference between the Germanic **i-umlaut** that gave English *foot/feet* and historical Indo-European **ablaut** is important: Neva currently copies the o/e case-stem alternation, **not** a plural-specific Germanic umlaut rule.

## Vowel policy to decide

1. Ban only awkward spelling like oe, keep ai/oi as diphthongs.
2. Remove phonemic diphthongs but allow adjacent vowels pronounced separately (hiatus).
3. Prefer single-vowel inflection endings and avoid most vowel sequences; revise lexical roots case by case rather than mechanically.

The user's preference appears closest to option 3, but is not yet ratified. **y is a single vowel /y/ (German ü)**, not a diphthong.
